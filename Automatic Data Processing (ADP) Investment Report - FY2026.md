# Automatic Data Processing, Inc. (ADP)

## Executive Stock Report

**Reporting basis:** ADP’s fiscal 2026 Form 10-K and the durable dividend compounder policy developed for this analysis  
**Fiscal year ended:** June 30, 2026  
**Prepared for:** Executive review

## Executive Assessment

Automatic Data Processing is a well-established, high-quality company built around services that employers cannot easily postpone. Businesses must pay their employees, file payroll taxes, administer benefits, maintain workforce records, and comply with employment regulations regardless of economic conditions. Because these services are essential, ADP benefits from largely recurring revenue and long-standing client relationships. Its competitive position is further supported by its scale, regulatory expertise, and the difficulty of changing payroll providers without disrupting operations.

These qualities make ADP a strong candidate for a durable dividend portfolio. The company combines dependable cash generation, an established dividend record, share repurchases, and moderate long-term growth. Its business model is also less sensitive to economic cycles than those of many other companies. The Employer Services segment’s 92.1% client revenue retention rate in fiscal 2026 provides further evidence of the durability of its customer relationships.

The investment case still has limitations. ADP is exposed to changes in employment levels and interest rates, cybersecurity threats, payroll-processing failures, and liabilities associated with its professional employer organization. The company’s increasing use of artificial intelligence also brings both competitive opportunities and additional regulatory and operational risks. Most importantly, even a strong company can be a poor investment if its shares are purchased at an excessive valuation.

**Recommendation:** Add ADP to the approved research list as a leading durable dividend compounder. Any purchase should remain subject to a current review of valuation, dividend coverage, leverage, and free cash flow.

## Company and Business Model

ADP provides cloud-based human capital management technology and outsourced workforce services. The company operates through two primary segments: Employer Services and Professional Employer Organization Services.

Employer Services provides payroll processing, payroll-tax filing, human resources administration, benefits administration, timekeeping, recruiting, talent management, retirement-plan services, insurance access, and regulatory-compliance support. Its primary platforms include RUN Powered by ADP for small businesses, ADP Workforce Now for midsized and large North American employers, and ADP Lyric HCM for large global organizations.

ADP TotalSource is the company’s professional employer organization. It provides clients with a broader outsourcing arrangement in which ADP and the client co-employ the client’s workers. Under this structure, ADP handles payroll, tax filings, benefits administration, compliance assistance, and workers’ compensation. The client remains responsible for managing its employees and operating the underlying business.

ADP earns revenue from recurring service fees, implementation fees, employee- or transaction-based charges, and interest earned on payroll and tax funds held temporarily before payment. This mix gives ADP several sources of income, although it also exposes the company to employment levels, interest rates, and risks associated with the accurate movement of client funds.

## Strategic Strengths

### Essential and Recurring Services

Payroll processing and tax filing are necessary business functions. Employers cannot simply suspend these services during an economic downturn. This creates recurring demand and gives ADP greater stability than businesses that depend heavily on discretionary purchases.

Changing payroll providers can also be expensive and disruptive. A new provider must transfer employee records, tax information, benefit elections, banking instructions, and compliance data without interrupting employee pay. These switching costs support ADP’s client retention and make its established customer relationships particularly valuable.

### Scale and Operating Reach

ADP pays approximately 26 million workers in the United States and moved more than $3.5 trillion in client funds during fiscal 2026. The company serves clients in more than 140 countries and territories. This scale allows ADP to invest heavily in technology, compliance, cybersecurity, and customer service. Smaller competitors may find it difficult to match those investments while offering the same range and depth of services.

### Broad Product Ecosystem

ADP can support a business from the hiring of its first employee through its development into a large multinational organization. Payroll services often establish the initial client relationship. ADP can then add benefits administration, scheduling, retirement-plan services, compliance support, talent management, and other outsourcing services. This broad product ecosystem creates opportunities to generate additional revenue from existing clients. It also makes the overall relationship more integrated and, therefore, more difficult to replace.

### Attractive Operating Evidence

Employer Services achieved a 92.1% client revenue retention rate in fiscal 2026. ADP also reported $4.230 billion in cash and cash equivalents as of June 30, 2026. Diluted earnings per share were $10.94, based on 403.3 million diluted weighted-average shares during the fiscal year.

These results reflect a large and profitable company with substantial liquidity and strong client continuity. The diluted weighted-average share figure represents an average for the full fiscal year and should not be confused with the number of shares outstanding on the final day of the reporting period.

## Dividend and Capital Allocation

ADP fits the central purpose of a durable dividend compounder strategy. It offers a combination of dividend consistency, dependable cash generation, business durability, and continued growth. Previous research identified more than 50 consecutive years of dividend growth, along with management’s stated objective of maintaining a dividend payout ratio of approximately 55% to 60% of earnings.

The company also has a history of repurchasing its shares. However, repurchases create value only when they reduce the diluted share count after employee stock compensation and when management purchases shares at reasonable prices. The investment review should therefore focus on the actual change in diluted weighted-average shares rather than the dollar value of an announced repurchase authorization.

ADP’s dividends and share repurchases should be evaluated as part of its overall capital-allocation policy. Both should remain supported by normalized free cash flow, and repurchases should not be financed through a sustained increase in leverage.

As of June 30, 2026, ADP reported $4.964 billion of long-term debt, no outstanding commercial paper, and $139.3 million of reverse repurchase obligations. Together, these borrowing-related balances totaled approximately $5.103 billion, excluding operating leases and obligations associated with client funds.

## Management Assessment

ADP’s leadership history reflects a high degree of internal continuity. Maria Black has worked at ADP since 1996 and has served as president and chief executive officer since January 2023. Peter Hadley joined ADP in 2002 and became chief financial officer in July 2025 after serving as corporate treasurer and, before that, as president of the company’s Asia-Pacific operations.

Joe DeSilva and David Foskett also moved into expanded operating and sales roles in January 2025 following long careers with the company. Samantha Orihuela, who joined ADP as chief marketing officer in February 2026, represents the most notable recent external addition to the executive leadership team.

This pattern suggests that ADP prefers to develop leaders internally while selectively adding outside expertise when needed. That approach supports organizational stability and helps preserve institutional knowledge. The Form 10-K provides the executives’ professional histories but does not explain the specific reasons behind their appointments. It would therefore be inappropriate to attribute these changes to retirement, performance concerns, or strategic disagreements without additional evidence.

## Principal Business Risks

### Client-Funds and Payment Risk

ADP handles exceptionally large amounts of money over relatively short settlement periods. An operating error, unauthorized transaction, bank failure, settlement delay, or client’s failure to provide the required funds could leave ADP responsible for payroll or tax payments. The scale of these financial transfers strengthens ADP’s competitive position, but it also increases the potential consequences of a control or processing failure.

### Cybersecurity and Privacy Risk

ADP stores payroll, banking, tax, benefits, and other sensitive personal information. This makes the company an attractive target for criminals seeking money or confidential data. ADP disclosed that it has experienced attempted or actual intrusions, malicious software, network disruptions, and denial-of-service activity. Identified incidents had not materially affected the company as of the filing date. Nevertheless, a serious future incident could result in financial losses, litigation, regulatory penalties, reputational damage, and client departures.

### Payroll-System Availability

ADP’s clients depend on the company to process payroll accurately and on schedule. Even a short system interruption could delay employee payments or tax remittances. The company also relies on banks, data centers, cloud platforms, and other third-party service providers. As a result, a failure involving an outside provider could quickly become an ADP service problem.

### PEO and Workers’ Compensation Exposure

ADP TotalSource serves more than 770,000 worksite employees through co-employment arrangements. This structure can expose ADP to obligations involving wages, payroll taxes, employee benefits, employment matters, and workers’ compensation. That exposure can include situations in which a client fails to provide the required funds. ADP reported approximately $390 million in standby letters of credit supporting certain workers’ compensation obligations.

The company also disclosed a proposed $48 million settlement related to alleged Employee Retirement Income Security Act violations involving the ADP TotalSource Retirement Savings Plan. ADP recorded the liability together with a $30 million expected insurance recovery, subject to court approval. This matter demonstrates that the PEO business introduces legal and fiduciary risks in addition to generating recurring revenue.

### Employment and Economic Sensitivity

Many ADP client contracts include fees based on the number of employees or transactions processed. A recession, widespread layoffs, or a structural decline in employment could reduce processing revenue. Lower employment could also reduce the amount of client funds ADP holds temporarily, decreasing the interest income earned from investing those balances.

### Interest-Rate and Investment Risk

ADP temporarily invests funds collected for client payroll and tax obligations. The income generated from these balances can be affected by interest rates, credit-market conditions, liquidity, changes in remittance rules, and the timing of employment-related cash flows. A significant market disruption could also force ADP to sell securities at a loss to meet short-term client obligations.

### Technology and Artificial Intelligence Risk

Artificial intelligence provides ADP with opportunities to automate tasks, improve its products, and generate useful insights from workforce data. At the same time, it introduces new competitive, legal, and regulatory uncertainty. New AI-based products could reduce the value of established human capital management systems. ADP’s own AI tools could also produce inaccurate, biased, or discriminatory results. Such outcomes could lead to litigation, regulatory intervention, reputational damage, or higher compliance costs.

### Regulatory Dependence

ADP operates within a broad set of payroll, tax, employment, benefits, privacy, banking, investment, and insurance regulations. Changes to payroll-tax remittance periods could reduce the income ADP earns from temporarily held client funds. Changes in the legal treatment of PEO co-employment arrangements or employee benefit plans could require the company to modify the TotalSource business model. New privacy and artificial intelligence regulations may also increase product-development and compliance expenses.

## External Research

### S&P Dow Jones Indices: Dividend Growth as a Quality Discipline

The S&P Dow Jones Indices research article, [*A Case for Dividend Growth Strategies*](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/research/article/a-case-for-dividend-growth-strategies/), argues that companies capable of maintaining and increasing their dividends can provide investors with exposure to higher-quality businesses and growing income over time. The research also presents dividend growth as a potential buffer against market volatility and, to some extent, rising interest-rate risk.

This evidence supports the report’s emphasis on dividend durability and growth rather than selecting stocks solely for their current yields. When applying this framework to ADP, the central question is whether its earnings and free cash flow can continue supporting a growing dividend through different economic conditions. ADP’s recurring payroll revenue and long history of dividend growth make it a logical candidate under this approach. However, the company must still satisfy the report’s payout, cash-flow, leverage, and valuation requirements.

The source is published by an index provider whose business includes dividend-focused indices. Its research is useful for understanding the strategy, but its conclusions should still be considered alongside independent analysis and the company’s regulatory filings.

### Morningstar: ADP’s Competitive Position

Morningstar’s equity research report, [*ADP Is a Payroll Powerhouse With a Wide Economic Moat*](https://www.morningstar.com/company-reports/1231379-adp-is-a-payroll-powerhouse-with-a-wide-economic-moat), classifies ADP as a wide-moat business. Morningstar also highlights ADP’s investment in cloud-native products and its consolidation of older, separate platforms as important parts of the company’s competitive development.

This assessment is consistent with the evidence presented in ADP’s Form 10-K. The company combines scale, regulatory expertise, integrated payroll and human resources products, and high client retention. Changing payroll providers requires the careful transfer of employee, tax, banking, benefits, and compliance information, creating meaningful switching costs. ADP’s investment in shared cloud platforms may also improve its ability to serve clients, introduce new features, and compete with newer software providers.

Morningstar’s moat rating strengthens the qualitative business case, but it should not be treated as a purchase signal by itself. Competitive advantages can weaken, and even a wide-moat company can produce disappointing returns if its shares are purchased at an excessive price. Access to Morningstar’s complete analyst report may also require a subscription.

### Effect on the Recommendation

The external research reinforces two important parts of the investment thesis:

1. The screening policy’s emphasis on sustainable dividend growth is supported by evidence linking dividend consistency with business quality and potentially lower volatility.
2. Independent equity research broadly supports the view that ADP has a durable competitive position based on its scale, integrated technology, and difficult-to-replace payroll relationships.

Neither source resolves the report’s most important remaining question: valuation. The recommendation therefore remains approved for further investment research, with any purchase subject to a current valuation and expected-return analysis.

## Alignment With the Investment Policy

ADP appears to satisfy the strategy’s qualitative requirements for an established large-cap company. It benefits from recurring demand, strong customer retention, pricing capacity, economies of scale, and relatively low dependence on discretionary spending. Its long dividend history, recurring revenue, and capital-light service model make ADP a stronger match for this strategy than many companies offering higher current dividend yields.

The company does not eliminate business-cycle risk. Employment levels affect transaction volumes, while interest rates influence the income earned from client funds. ADP should therefore be viewed as economically resilient rather than economically immune.

Several policy requirements still need to be calculated or updated with current market information before a purchase can receive final approval:

- Current dividend yield compared with the required range of 1.5% to 4.5%.
- Earnings and free-cash-flow payout ratios.
- Five-year free-cash-flow-per-share growth.
- Net debt to EBITDA and interest coverage.
- Net reduction in diluted shares after accounting for stock-based compensation.
- Current valuation compared with ADP’s historical price-to-earnings ratio, enterprise value to EBIT, and dividend yield.
- A conservative estimate of the expected annual return over the next five years.

## Board Recommendation

ADP should move forward to the valuation and portfolio-construction stage. The company possesses the primary characteristics sought under this strategy: necessary and recurring services, long-standing client relationships, high retention, substantial cash generation, an established dividend, and a record of returning capital to shareholders.

The main concern is the price investors must pay for those qualities. ADP is widely recognized as a durable company, and the market may assign its shares a premium valuation. Strong management and a stable business cannot compensate for an entry price that already assumes unrealistic future growth.

Approval to purchase should require confirmation that free cash flow adequately supports the dividend and share repurchases, leverage remains within the policy limit, and the expected annual return reaches at least 8% under conservative assumptions. If those conditions are met, ADP would be suitable as a core holding within a diversified portfolio of 20 to 30 dividend compounders, subject to the proposed initial position limit of 5%.

## Proposed Decision

**Status:** Approved for additional investment research; purchase remains subject to valuation review.

**Investment role:** Core durable dividend compounder.

**Primary reason to own:** ADP provides essential and recurring payroll and workforce services supported by high client retention, substantial scale, and a long history of dividend growth.

**Primary reason to wait:** The available research does not yet demonstrate that the current share price provides the required expected return or an adequate margin of safety.
